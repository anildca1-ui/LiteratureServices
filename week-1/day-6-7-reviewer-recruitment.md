# Day 6–7 — Reviewer recruitment (materials)

Goal per the plan: recruit **three** native-reviewer candidates and pay each
₹1,500–2,000 for the same real task — keyword validation + final read on the
mock sweep. Paying all three for identical work is the audition; you keep
the best one at Rs 1,500–2,000 per job.

## Where to look (in order of practicality)

1. **Chinese-language programmes in India** — MA/advanced-diploma students
   and recent graduates: JNU (Delhi), EFL University (Hyderabad — local),
   Doon University, Visva-Bharati, Central University of Gujarat. Route:
   department notice boards, alumni groups, a polite email to the
   department office with the gig notice below. Native or near-native
   readers with technical comfort are the filter, not certificates.
2. **Freelance platforms** — Upwork/ProZ, filter: native Chinese, patent or
   technical translation experience, comfortable being paid per-task.
   Overseas reviewers (Malaysia, Singapore, Taiwan) avoid India–China
   payment friction; pay via Wise/PayPal. Mainland reviewers: per-document
   reimbursement structure per the master brief.
3. **Chinese nationals studying/working in India** — university
   international-student offices, city WeChat groups (ask contacts for an
   intro; don't cold-spam groups).

## Gig notice (copy-paste)

> **Part-time: Chinese patent-document reviewer (remote, per-task)**
>
> I run a technical-document research desk in Hyderabad. I need a native
> Chinese reader for short, well-defined tasks: (1) review a list of
> Chinese technical search terms for a given engineering concept — mark
> wrong/unnatural terms, add missing colloquial ones (~15 min); (2) read
> 5–8 short published patent documents against a checklist I provide and
> flag where the machine translation misleads (~1 hr).
>
> No legal work, no confidential client material — you only ever see
> published documents and generic technical concepts. Engineering
> background or comfort with technical text preferred. ₹1,500–2,000 per
> task-set [adjust for overseas: state in USD/RMB equivalent], paid per
> task. Ongoing work if the first tasks go well.
>
> Reply with: your background, one line on your technical comfort area,
> and your rate expectation.

## Audition = the mock sweep, scored

Give all three candidates the same two tasks from the Day 5 sweep:

- **Task A — keyword validation:** the D1+DeepSeek union list. Score:
  did they catch the planted dud term (add one deliberately wrong term
  before sending); how many genuinely colloquial terms did they add;
  did they explain *why* a term is wrong or just delete it?
- **Task B — final read:** top 5 shortlisted documents + the frozen
  relevance criteria. Score: did they catch MT inversions/garbles the D2
  triage flagged; do their relevance calls agree with each other's
  (inter-reviewer agreement is your quality signal); turnaround discipline.

Keep score in a simple table; pick one primary + one backup. Tell the
third they're on the bench for overflow — a solo business needs
redundancy, and "reviewer is sick" is failure reason 3 territory
(`day-1-red-team.md`).

## Boundaries (from the data-handling statement — hold them from day one)

The reviewer never sees: client names, matter context, full briefs, or
anything unpublished. They see abstracted keyword lists and published
documents only. Set this expectation in the very first email — it is part
of the product you sell.
