# Day 1 — Red team: the three strongest reasons this plan fails

Executed per the Day 1 instruction: *"argue against this plan; give me the
three strongest reasons it fails, and what evidence in the first 30 days
would confirm each. Be ruthless."*

---

## Failure reason 1 — The long tail doesn't buy: "good enough for free" wins

The plan's honest weakness section already concedes that Google Patents and
Espacenet index CN utility models with free English MT. The bet is that a
10–30-person Indian search shop, billing thin margins on fixed-fee projects,
will pay Rs 15–18K per matter for a *supplement* to a search they already
consider done. That is a luxury purchase in a price-brutal market. These
shops win work by being cheaper than Gurgaon's big names; their clients
rarely audit recall. The delta can be real and still not be bought —
you are selling insurance against a risk the buyer's own client never
prices. And T2's real buyers (List B exporters) have longer sales cycles
and procurement layers the plan's week-count doesn't model.

**30-day confirming evidence:** response rate under ~4% across 100+ List A
contacts; meetings that agree the delta is interesting but end with "send us
your rate card, we'll call when a matter needs it" and never a vendor form;
every price conversation anchoring to Rs 5K, not Rs 15K.

**What would disconfirm it:** even 2–3 firms completing vendor registration
and one paid audition inside 30 days.

## Failure reason 2 — The supply chain never graduates from the wallet

The whole business stands on a retail pay-per-view wallet the plan itself
calls a bridge: no purchase invoices, no GST input, freeze risk, and a
month-4 deadline to land CNKI/Wanfang agent terms or a mainland partner.
CNKI has no visible incentive to onboard a solo Indian reseller — its
overseas commercial motion targets institutions, and post-2023 it is more
cautious, not less. The mainland-partner alternative requires trust,
a HK/SG invoicing entity, and India–China remittance workarounds — each a
project in itself. Meanwhile B2B clients will ask for invoices your bridge
cannot produce. The kill criterion exists on paper; the risk is drifting
past it because revenue trickles in.

**30-day confirming evidence:** no substantive reply from CNKI International
or Wanfang after two follow-ups; wallet friction on the very first test
purchase (top-up blocked, identity verification wall, per-day caps); a
prospect asking "can you give a GST invoice for the source cost?" with no
good answer.

**What would disconfirm it:** any commercial conversation opened with either
supplier, or a credible mainland/HK procurement contact identified and
tested with one paid document.

## Failure reason 3 — The trust ceiling: solo vendor, litigation-adjacent work

Prior-art work touches live disputes. Even mid-size firms run vendor
onboarding: NDAs are the easy part; the hard parts are "who else sees our
brief," E&O expectations, references, and continuity risk ("what if he's
sick mid-matter?"). The mitigations (mutual NDA, data-handling statement,
abstraction rule) are genuinely good — but they answer the objection at the
*rational* layer, while vendor selection at these firms is mostly a
*career-risk* decision: nobody gets fired for not adding an unknown solo
vendor. The audition-at-cost offer may not clear that bar, and the reviewer
dependency (a part-time native reader you haven't hired yet) is a single
point of failure a diligent client will probe.

**30-day confirming evidence:** interested replies that die precisely at the
vendor-registration/security-questionnaire stage; more than one prospect
proposing you work as an untracked subcontractor under *their* NDA at half
your price; failure to sign even one reviewer with verifiable competence by
day 30.

**What would disconfirm it:** one firm accepting your NDA + data-handling
pack as-is, or a warm referral (Define IPR route) converting to a paid job —
referrals are how the career-risk barrier actually falls.

## Secondary risks (real, but weaker than the three above)

- **Founder time-math:** 60/40 outreach/delivery collapses the first week a
  real T3 job lands next to a T2+ landscape. Watch the D7 time-split number.
- **AI melt is faster than 12–24 months:** if frontier models close the
  native-keyword recall gap, T3's window shortens; T1/T2 provenance value
  survives. The mock sweep partially tests this — see Day 5 design.

## What Day 1–30 must therefore prioritise

The three confirming-evidence lists above are the *actual* Week 1–4 metrics
dashboard. Track them explicitly in the Friday D7 review, next to the

right responses; the danger is not reading the evidence honestly.
